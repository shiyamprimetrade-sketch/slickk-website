# SLICKK conversion review

Prepared 28 October 2026. These are visitor hypotheses and review criteria, not recorded interviews, experiments or measured conversion improvements. Validate them with actual feedback and consent-respecting observation.

## Visitor hypotheses A–G

| Visitor | Likely decision | Friction to remove | Appropriate next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — First-time small-business owner | Can I afford a useful website? | Unclear starting price, hidden extras or technical jargon | Pricing → website budget guide → scoped inquiry |
| B — Existing business refreshing its identity | Will the work fit real business applications? | Confusing a logo with a complete identity; unspecified exports | Identity service → labelled concepts → identity brief |
| C — Corporate buyer | Can I understand scope and responsibility? | Unsupported client claims, unclear delivery process | Services → process and exclusions → written proposal request |
| D — Founder planning custom software | Can this solve my actual workflow? | Broad automation promises; unclear permissions or dependencies | Software service → project brief guide → discovery inquiry |
| E — Mobile visitor comparing options | Can I find the relevant service quickly? | Dense navigation, illegible text or obscured action buttons | Service selection → concise mobile form |
| F — International prospect | Can I work with a Bangladesh-based independent practice? | Invented overseas presence, unclear handover or currency | About → scope and BDT pricing context → inquiry |
| G — Cautious visitor checking legitimacy | Are the claims and contact route trustworthy? | Fake reviews, unverified social links or ambiguous concept work | About, policies and honest portfolio labels → verified contact route |

## Shared journey

The opening screen should explain the service category and offer a clear project action. A visitor should be able to choose a service, inspect scope or examples, understand starting prices where available, and submit a brief without repeating their selection. Keep browsing actions available for visitors who are not ready to inquire.

Package cards must distinguish starting prices from final proposals. Show exclusions close to the price. Concept work demonstrates direction, not actual client results. Preserve those labels on detail pages and previews. Do not publish unverified WhatsApp numbers, social profiles, client relationships, ratings or outcome metrics.

## Form and trust review criteria

- Preserve the selected service in the inquiry journey; allow visitors to change it.
- Use visible labels and useful inline errors. Keep entered values after a recoverable submission failure.
- Explain why contact information is requested, link the privacy notice and state the actual inquiry destination accurately.
- Show success only after the server accepts the inquiry. Do not imply email delivery or a response-time promise unless operationally confirmed.
- Verify keyboard access, focus visibility, touch targets and form completion on a narrow screen. Verify reduced-motion behavior for decorative effects.
- Keep bot protection and private inbox access working. A public success message must never expose another inquiry or an administrator credential.
- Offer the verified business email where appropriate; keep unsupported messaging channels unpublished.

## Objection handling through content

The website-cost guide clarifies the BDT 10,000 starting package, content responsibility and recurring costs. The identity guide distinguishes the BDT 2,000 logo, BDT 8,000 identity and BDT 5,000 brochure scopes. The software guide explains roles, acceptance checks and external dependencies without inventing a fixed development price. Each guide ends with an appropriately selected inquiry action.

## Validation and measurement boundaries

Before release, the integrated project must verify navigation, service preselection, successful and failed form states, responsive layout and private-access boundaries. These are acceptance criteria, not a claim that tests have passed in this document.

After release, observe where consenting visitors open service pages, inspect pricing and begin inquiries. Compare those signals with real, qualified inquiries over an appropriate observation period. Small counts, absent consent and missing traffic attribution limit conclusions. Do not describe a redesign as a measured conversion lift without comparable evidence.

Owner decisions still needed include any response-time commitment, verified public messaging accounts, permission to publish real work and email-notification sender setup. Until confirmed, retain honest neutral wording and the existing inquiry-storage workflow.
